Define the experiment

Saving propensity is the share of income left after essential costs that is retained. This differs from the original cash-trade cap.

Grid, population and seed

Every income distribution has the same exact sample mean. Its finite-sample Gini is calibrated to the column. Income ranks stay fixed during a run.

Returns, growth and tax

Returns apply before each monthly contribution. Risk is independent across households and months. Income tax applies to earned income; payments and investment returns are untaxed.

Differences within each cell

With a saving spread, row labels are central propensities; individual rates are clipped to 0–100%. The cell inspector reports the realised mean. Initial wealth represents starting capital, not a generation-by-generation inheritance process.

Model, financial identities and interpretation

What happens each month

  1. Apply the selected real investment return to existing wealth.
  2. Receive earned income, deduct flat income tax, and add the universal payment.
  3. Pay essential costs. If income cannot cover them, draw on existing wealth. Record any remaining unmet cost; borrowing is unavailable.
  4. Save the row’s fraction of remaining income. Consume the rest.

wealth next = wealth + investment gain + net income + payment − consumption

All money amounts are model units in constant purchasing power. No inflation adjustment is needed. Earned income and universal payments enter from outside the household sector; tax and consumption leave it. Tax receipts do not finance the universal payment in this model.

The accounting inspector checks the cumulative identity: initial wealth + earned income + universal payments + returns − tax − consumption = final wealth. Unmet essentials are an unfinanced need, not hidden debt or expenditure.

Saving is not an exchange cap

This module uses a retained fraction of income after essentials. The original exchange experiment caps each payment as a fraction of cash holdings. They are separate models; their numerical propensities are not interchangeable.

Income and uncertainty

Each replication draws a lognormal-shaped set of permanent incomes. A numerical calibration fixes its exact mean and Gini. Columns change inequality while holding total income fixed. They do not increase the average income; use the mean-income control for that.

The same underlying draws, initial wealth and monthly return shocks are shared across cells within a replication. Replications use distinct deterministic seeds. Cell intervals are Student-t 95% Monte Carlo intervals for the mean run-level outcome. Paired comparisons use within-replication differences. These intervals do not quantify uncertainty about the assumptions or about real-world households.

With no costs, no returns, no starting wealth and a common positive saving propensity, final wealth equals income × saving propensity × months. Changing a common propensity scales wealth but leaves its Gini unchanged. The pure-accumulation preset makes that identity testable. Gini is undefined when all wealth is zero; the grid displays an em dash.

Boundaries of this version

Incomes and costs are specified externally. There are no firms, debt, housing assets, unemployment, progressive taxes, tax-funded transfers or intergenerational succession. Financial shocks are investment-return shocks. Rates of return have the same expected value for everyone. The sensitivity summary describes the tested parameter ranges; it does not assign causal shares of real-world inequality.
